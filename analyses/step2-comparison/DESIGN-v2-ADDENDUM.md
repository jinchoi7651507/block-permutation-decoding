### 1b. Power cells (§15 "Power")
- Pilot: primary-cell layout, P5 only, δ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8}, 50 replicates each, seeds 2,027,000 + i (`run_sim_cells.py --power-pilot`).
- Choice (`code/choose_delta.py`, fixed before the pilot): d_mid = smallest δ with P5 power in [0.20, 0.50]; d_large = smallest δ with power in [0.70, 0.90]; if no δ falls in a band, the δ closest to it (ties: smaller δ); if both pick the same δ, d_large = the next larger δ.
- Main: 1,000 replicates, all rules, at d_mid and d_large, seeds 2,027,100 and 2,027,101 (`--power d_mid,d_large`).
- Size-adjusted power: each rule's threshold q is the 5% quantile (lower) of its p in the δ = 0 primary cell (cell 0), with P3 errors and NaN counted as p = 1. Permutation p values move in steps of 1/201, so an inflated rule can have more than 5% of its null replicates at the threshold; the test is therefore randomised at the tie: reject if p < q, and if p = q with probability g = (0.05 − P0(p < q)) / P0(p = q), which makes the expected size exactly 5%. Reported per rule: raw power, q, the realised size of the plain rule p ≤ q, g, and the expected size-adjusted power P1(p < q) + g·P1(p = q) (`summarize_sim.size_adjusted`).

## 2. Allen cells (`code/allen_cells.py`)
- Data, units and areas as step 1 (32 sessions, six visual areas, units Allen labels good, rate ≥ 0.5 Hz). A session is skipped if it has fewer than 3 spontaneous epochs of at least 240 s (as step 1). Spontaneous schemes need at least 20 units in both the 12-block and the 24-block counts, drifting-grating schemes at least 20 units (as step 1). Every scheme asserts 24 blocks. Primary estimates over the 30 new mice; all 32 as sensitivity.
- Pipeline and rules: `sim.pipeline()` and the five rules of `sim.py`, 200 permutation draws. LogisticRegression non-convergence is counted per row.
- **Splits:** S-main (shuffled KFold 5) and S-tool (unshuffled StratifiedKFold 5, re-split per permuted label vector), as §15; **beyond §15 (author decision 2026-10-21):** S-block (GroupKFold(5) by the 24 blocks, deterministic) for every scheme, and "pair" (the step 1 pair split in one direction, as step 1: train on the calibration-pair blocks, test on the held-out-pair blocks) for `sep24` and `dg_sep24`; under "pair" only the held-out half is predicted, so P1 there uses the number of held-out trials as n (the stamped `sim.p1_binomial` counts all trials). S-block is the only real-noise test of the separate-block condition (§4 variant (b), §11 "S-block: trial-level permutation on block folds above 5%").
- **P3 runs:** the recording's natural runs: the 3 spontaneous epochs (8 blocks each) for `shared24`/`sep24`; the Allen stimulus blocks of the drifting-grating presentations for `dg_*` (number of runs stored per row; areas with a single run are counted in the summary); 4 runs of 6 blocks for `pos_dg24` (reordered trials).
- **Schemes:**
  - `shared24`: spontaneous activity, 2-s pseudo-trials, 3 epochs × 8 blocks (step 1 layout); 12 of 24 blocks label 1.
  - `sep24`: same data; 6 of the 12 calibration-pair blocks and 6 of the 12 held-out-pair blocks label 1 (step 1 pair split). In step 1 the pair split was also the training/test split, so a held-out block's label was independent of the training blocks' labels. Under S-main, S-tool and S-block every split mixes the two halves, so `sep24` is there a design that differs from `shared24` only in its balance constraint; those rows are not counted as evidence independent of `shared24`. Under the "pair" split it reproduces the step 1 separation. The labels are new draws (streams + 200), not step 1's labels.
  - `dg_shared24`, `dg_sep24`: drifting-grating presentations in time order cut into 24 blocks (step 1 layout), same labels as above.
  - `pos_dg24` (positive control, §5b): real 0° and 90° labels. The 0° presentations (in time order) are cut into 12 chunks and the 90° presentations into 12 chunks, and the chunks alternate (0°, 90°, …) as 24 blocks; chunk j of each label covers about the same recording time. A pipeline check at ceiling, not power of the rules. The reordered sequence is not in time order, which matters for S-tool's contiguous folds.
- P5 null set: the scheme's own assignment set; under these random assignments P5 is exact by randomisation (sanity cell), so contrast R is each rule's rate minus about 5%.
- Seeds: labels `tax.rng_for(session, area, stream)` with the step 1 streams + 200 (shared24 224, sep24 222, dg_shared24 314, dg_sep24 312); rules and folds stream + 1,000 × (split index + 1) (main 0, tool 1, block 2, pair 3); `pos_dg24` stream 470. The same label vector is used under every split (paired on labels).
- Stored per (session, area, scheme, split): p of every rule, P3 error flag, observed accuracy (mean of fold accuracies), the 200 P5 null accuracies, non-convergence count.
- Run after the simulation grid (shared cores) and after this addendum's timestamp is attested. About 154 areas × (4 schemes × 3 splits + 2 pair + pos_dg24 × 3) ≈ 2,600 cells × ~15 s ≈ 11 core-hours.

## 3. Simulation summary (`code/summarize_sim.py`)
- Per cell and rule: replicates (and planned replicates), rate of p < 0.05, Wilson 95% interval, the Monte Carlo 99% band of 5% at that number of replicates. NaN p = error, excluded from the rate, counted.
- P3: error share, rate among non-errors, rate with errors as non-rejections, P5 on the same surviving (finite-P3) replicates.
- **Contrast M** (primary cell): per replicate hit(Pk) − hit(P5), mean and 95% bootstrap over replicates (B 10,000, seed 20261022); for P3 both on survivors and with errors as non-rejections on all replicates. P5 is 5% by construction in the random design, so M is each rule's rate minus about 5% (Monte Carlo SE about 0.7 points at 1,000 replicates).
- **Baselines:** primary = pooled interleaved drift-0 rate over the four τ_s copies (2,000 replicates) for the same B and split; second, descriptive = pooled random-design drift-0 rate excluding the compared cell's own rows (under S-tool and S-block the label structure also changes the fold structure). Each "minus baseline" difference has a 95% bootstrap interval and both denominators. Trend-0.5 cells are compared with the trend-0 baseline.
- **Q3:** primary cell = interleaved, τ_s 20, drift 1, B 12, S-main, with its 99% band; the other interleaved cells are descriptive.
- **P5 fails** (fixed order, trend 0) = rate above the upper end of the 99% band (one-sided, about 0.5% per cell, 12 cells); rates below the lower end are reported descriptively.
- **B = 4:** 6 balanced assignments, and an assignment and its swap give the same accuracy, so P5 has 3 distinct null values and its smallest attainable p is about 1/3. Printed as "cannot reject" with the smallest p seen. **Correction to `DESIGN.md` §15**, which says "smallest p ≈ 0.17".

## 4. Allen summary and group-level column (`code/summarize_allen.py`)
- Rows deduplicated on (session, area, scheme, split); the number of areas per scheme × split is reported.
- Mouse-level rates with 95% mouse bootstrap (B 20,000, seed as step 1; the same resampling indices in every cell, so intervals are marginal), per scheme × split × rule; P3 also with errors as non-rejections, and the share of areas with a P3 error.
- **Contrast R:** `shared24`, S-main, per area hit(Pk) − hit(P5), averaged per mouse, mouse bootstrap.
- **Group-level column (§6), as rates:** the 200 stored P5 null accuracies of each area are 200 refitted fake-label experiments drawn from the scheme's own set. For each null index j, the j-th null accuracy of every area plays the observed accuracy; across mice: (a) one-sample t-test of the per-mouse mean against 0.5, (b) sign-flip permutation (1,000 flips), (c) a valid group test whose group null is built from the other 199 null accuracies (1,000 draws: mean over mice of the per-mouse mean of one null accuracy per area). Reported: rejection rates of (a), (b), (c) over the 200 experiments, and the observed experiment's three p values. Per-mouse means use the areas with a finite accuracy, in the experiment and in the null alike. (c) is close to 5% by construction (a sanity check); the result is the rate of (a) and (b). (c) is a fixed-effects (global-null) test for these mice, not a test of a population mean (Allefeld et al. 2016). Splits main, tool and block. The simulation runner stores p values only, so there is no simulation group-level column; the EEG group level is in step 2b.
